# Interpreting activity outcomes in a parent review

## What the sources support

CDC guidance for parents of toddlers and preschoolers recommends choosing a few observable behaviors and naming the action when giving praise. A parent who sees “put dishes in the sink” has a concrete action to acknowledge. [CDC guidance on praise and rewards](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/using-rewards.html)

NAEYC explains that children can demonstrate a skill differently across settings and occasions. Its assessment guidance for early childhood educators calls for multiple sources of information when decisions have substantial consequences for a child. A household activity log captures one setting and the observations a parent chose to record. [NAEYC position statement on observation and assessment](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/position-statements/dap/assessing-development/)

## Implications for Daily Stars

These are product design inferences. Review one child and one named activity over a stated period. For example, “Put dishes in the sink · 5 scheduled evenings · 3 done correctly · 1 needs improvement · 1 unrecorded” gives a parent an action to discuss and makes the gap visible. If showing a percentage, describe 3 of 4 as the share of **recorded outcomes** marked done correctly. With zero recorded outcomes, show the count alone. Group by activity date and use the latest effective outcome so a later correction appears on the evening it describes. [Domain definitions](../../GLOSSARY.md)

A change in stars can reflect a new reward, a different assignment, or a one-off entry. To explore a change, compare the same child’s recorded outcomes for the same activity across two periods, including how many scheduled occurrences each period contains. A change in those counts can prompt a parent to ask what helped. The cited guidance provides no validated cutoff for mastery or decline in this tracker. ## Agreed report behavior

- Parents review one child at a time and choose a routine to praise or practice. Use wording they can discuss directly with the child.
- Default to the last seven completed household calendar days. Offer the last four weeks (28 completed days). Compare each activity with the immediately preceding period of the same length.
- Show done correctly, needs improvement, and unrecorded counts, together with recorded versus scheduled opportunities in each period. Display “Not scheduled” when there were no opportunities and “0 of 4 recorded” when four opportunities went unrecorded.
- Follow the same activity within its template across renames. A newly created activity starts its own history, even when it shares a name with an older activity.
- For each activity date, show routine points, one-off points, and net stars so a parent can explain a change in earnings.
